## 1b. Repair revision (this pass)

The first authored environment did not survive an independent capture: a flat
green upper third, a hard horizontal waterbed seam, repeated jagged stems/UV
strips and broken leaves. Root cause, from the shader maths and the two source
paintings:

* `LadyLake_Env_Waterbed` re-drew the painting's lakebed band from a *second*
  dune mesh with a different tint, which produced the seam and a doubled bed.
* `LadyLake_Env_WaterbedCaustics`, `..._CausticSheen` and `..._SunRays` were all
  full-frame **additive** layers, and the additive shader is `Blend One One`, so
  three of them stacking lifted the entire water column into flat green.
* The kelp/lily meshes sampled three tiny sub-rects of `foreground.png` into tall
  thin ribbons, tinted them green and let `_MainU/_MainV` **scroll** the art
  inside them. That is what read as "repeated jagged stems/UV strips".
* The backdrop plate was authored at x ±2.95 / y ±3.95, i.e. 19 % / 11 % larger
  than the 497x710 painting, so the art was both cropped and non-uniformly
  stretched.

The repair keeps the whole pipeline (original DynamicCards shaders, original
Aeschna textures, production animated-material component, 12 s Animator) and
replaces the geometry/compositing plan:

* **One plate per painting.** `Env_Backdrop` and `Env_Foreground` are full-frame
  relief grids whose mesh UV *is* the painting's own normalised coordinate, so
  `background.png` and `foreground.png` are reproduced 1:1 and stay registered
  with the model rig. The plate runs 0.4 % past the frustum and mirrors its UV
  outside the 497x710 frame, so the thin cover ring is a seamless continuation of
  the art rather than the camera clear colour. **The duplicate waterbed plate is
  gone**, which removes the seam at its root.
* **Accents are gated by the art.** `WaterbedCaustics` and `CausticSheen` are
  full-frame additive plates whose vertex alpha is baked at build time from
  `background.png`'s **own luminance** (times a vertical zone for the bed). Dark
  water gets no light and bright water gets a gentle shimmer, so the layers have
  no boundary anywhere to seam, and the painting is never washed out.
* **The plants are the painting, not ribbons.** `Env_Foreground` uses the
  original wavey foliage shader with a **white** tint, `_MainU = _MainV = 0` (the
  art can no longer slide) and a generated `Env_SwayMask.png` derived from the
  plant painting's own alpha: only the stems carry the vertex wave, the lily pads
  stay rigid.

## 5. Twelve second effect timing

Three cooperating, non-overlapping drivers, all on the same 12 s clock:

1. **Animator** (`LadyLakeEnvironment.controller`) — states `Intro` (12 s) →
   `Loop` (12 s, `m_LoopTime` set, exit-time transition, no conditions). Both
   clips are baked from the same envelope: `Sway_Rays` (2 cycles, ±0.55° and
   ±0.022 x), `Sway_Plants` (2 cycles, ±0.45°, ±0.030 x, ±0.018 y), and `m_Float`
   on the production `DynamicCardAnimatedMaterialProperty` (2 cycles, 0.62…0.95).
   Integer cycle counts, and the t=0 sine zero crossing baked into the prefab rest
   pose, make the seam continuous. The water motion itself comes from the shaders
   (noise-scrolled UVs and the wavey vertex displacement), not from sliding the
   paintings around.
2. **Production material path** — `MaterialAnimation` carries
   `Assets.Script.DynamicCards.DynamicCardAnimatedMaterialProperty`
   (`Type=2` float, `Name="_AlphaMult"`, `Slot=0`, `Targets=[WaterbedCaustics]`),
   which is exactly the component the imported premiums animate. It writes a
   `MaterialPropertyBlock`, never a material instance.
3. **`LadyLakeEnvironmentCycle`** (runtime, `Environment/Runtime/`) — the two
   luminous beats: sharp catch glint at **3.2 s** (40 ms attack, 160 ms decay,
   two ringing reflections) and the **5.6–8.3 s** lift swell, plus the full-frame
   sheen. `EvaluateGlow` is a pure function so the envelope is verifiable without
   play mode. The halo rides the model rig's `GripSocket` (fallback `Grip`);
   if neither exists it sits on the contract grip anchor
   (pixel 380,543 → `(1.315, -1.88, -0.20)`, just in front of the blade plane).
   Integration's `LadyLakeProductionEffects.Bake` replaces this component with
   `GlowAnimation`/`SheenAnimation` drivers in the assembled card; the builder's
   `glowIdle .10` / `causticIdle .26` match that bake exactly so the un-baked
   prefab (the environment-only capture) looks the way production will.

Effect events are exposed in `Environment/catalog-fragment.json`
(`particleEvents` restart `Bubbles`/`Motes`/`Glints` on the shared 12 s boundary,
`uvMotions` scroll the two caustics surfaces through the production `_ST` path).
Paths are relative to the Environment prefab root; Integration/root must prefix
them with the instance path inside `Card.prefab`.

## 7. Honest parity differences and remaining uncertainty

1. **Original meshes were not reused; original materials/shaders/textures were.**
   The Aeschna layer meshes in `…/Shared/15010100_1643xx__N_*.asset` are
   `SkinnedMeshRenderer` meshes whose bind pose and bone chain live inside the
   Aeschna `Card.prefab`. Instancing them under a `MeshFilter` does not reliably
   reproduce the source layout, and the Aeschna composition is a different
   painting from the selected `c10000000` art. The task explicitly allows
   "Unity native procedural mesh creation", so the geometry is real authored 3D
   geometry in the shared frame, while the **shader + texture + parameter** path
   is the original premium path. Parity difference: the silhouettes are ours,
   the look pipeline is the original one.
2. **Two original materials could not be reused byte-for-byte.** Their
   `_MainTex` is the Aeschna card atlas (`15010100_23464_15010100.png`), whose
   UV regions only make sense on the Aeschna mesh. New materials therefore
   reproduce their *parameters* but bind the selected art / copy of the original
   tileable texture instead. Parameters are documented above so a reviewer can
   diff them against the `.mat` files.
3. **New materials were created, no new shader.** All four shaders are original.
   The materials are new asset files (unavoidable, since a `.mat` cannot be
   edited without touching `DynamicCards/Content`, which is read-only here).
4. **The bubble material's texture slots are back to the source arrangement.**
   The source `15010100_164274__15010300_Aeschna_FX_Bubbles.mat` binds `_MainTex`
   = `963cbb…png` (the bubble sheet) and `_NoiseTex` = the tiling noise, and
   `LadyLake_Env_Bubbles.mat` now does the same: the bubble shape comes from the
   sheet's own alpha plus the authored vertex-alpha falloff, and the noise only
   jitters the UV. `LadyLake_Env_Motes.mat` uses the same sheet at a lower
   `_AlphaMult` and higher `_AlphaPower` for dust, and `LadyLake_Env_Glint.mat`
   uses the caustics tile additively for sparkle.
5. **No Unity render was produced by this worker.** The layer alphas were tuned
   numerically. To reduce the risk that created last time, the exact compositing
   path was reproduced on the CPU outside Unity — the two plates' UV mapping and
   mirror ring, the additive shaders' `_ColorGain * tint * tex.rgb * alpha` with
   `alpha = _AlphaMult * tint.a * tex.a^_AlphaPower * vcol.a`, and the straight
   alpha plant composite, all in Gamma space — and checked against the
   `background.png` + `foreground.png` composite at the capture's resolution. The
   numbers are in §3.4. This is a simulation of the authored data, not a Unity
   capture: the root's render is still the only visual acceptance.
6. **Particle appearance is unverified.** The bubbles/motes use authored,
   domed disc meshes with a vertex-alpha falloff (shape from geometry, not a
   runtime procedural effect), rendered with `RenderMode.Mesh` +
   `View` alignment and the original shaders. The combination was not observed.
   Particles do not simulate in edit mode, so they contribute nothing to a still
   capture either way.
7. **The plant painting is used whole**, so there are no hand-picked UV sub-rects
   left to get wrong. The generated `Env_SwayMask.png` is reproduced and inspected
   numerically (mean 0.31; ≈0.00 on the lily pad band, 0.78 on the bottom fan),
   but its in-editor appearance was not observed.
8. **The seam of the audio loop is crossfaded, not phase-continuous.** With
   ambience-grade material this is inaudible in principle, but no listening test
   was performed by this worker.
9. **Sources are assumed non-speech.** They are the premium card soundtracks
   (`catalog.json` `audio`), and the numeric profile (modulation 0.33, no silence
   gaps, stereo) matches ambience, but no speech classifier was run.
10. **`Build()` dirties the currently open scene** while it constructs the
    temporary `Environment` GameObject (it is destroyed immediately afterwards).
    This is the standard editor-builder pattern; invoke it from Unity with a
    saved or throwaway scene.
